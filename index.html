Line

Subway navigation that helps international professionals new to New York avoid unforced errors on their commute, from the first step underground to the right corner above it.

The problem

The line we heard most in interviews with New Yorkers: “People don’t know where to go in the subway.” For someone in their first week at a new job, a wrong entrance, a compass-style “Exit NW” or a dead signal underground turns an ordinary delay into arriving late.

472
subway stations
4.0M
weekday riders (MTA, 2025)
46%
of NYC’s workforce are immigrants

What we designed

Line numbers every subway entrance and exit, then guides riders through each checkpoint: the entrance for the right platform, the car and door nearest their exit, a buzz the moment they head the wrong way, and the exit to take, with left and right turns instead of compass points. It works offline, in the rider’s own language, and can project arrows onto the station floor through the phone camera or smart glasses.

3 of 3 riders who tested the concept chose Line as the most helpful option, over Exit Strategy and the MTA app.

What the research changed

  1. Compass directions fail newcomers.

    So Line says “turn left,” never “go NW.”

  2. The first mistake happens at the entrance.

    So Line routes riders to the numbered entrance for their platform.

  3. Newcomers already trust numbered exits.

    So Line brings the system riders know from Tokyo and Seoul to New York.

  4. Small errors compound into misery.

    So Line removes the avoidable errors, and a delay stays just a delay.

  5. Guidance has to live on the rider’s own device.

    So Line runs on the phone and pairs with riders’ own watches and glasses.
